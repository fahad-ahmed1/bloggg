How Long Does a Divorce Take in California? A Step-by-Step Timeline

One of the first questions people ask is simple: "How long is this going to take?" The honest answer is that it depends, but California's process follows a predictable path. Knowing the steps helps you plan your finances, your living arrangements and your expectations.

The six-month rule

California has a mandatory waiting period. A divorce cannot be finalized until at least six months after the other spouse has been served with the divorce papers (or has formally responded). Even if you and your spouse agree on everything, that clock must run. This is why even the smoothest case cannot be completed overnight.

The main steps, in order

  1. Prepare and file the petition. One spouse files the initial forms with the Superior Court in the county where residency requirements are met. Accurate at this stage prevents rejections that add weeks to the process.
  2. Serve your spouse. The other spouse must be formally given the papers by someone other than you. The six-month clock starts from service.
  3. Allow time to respond. The served spouse generally has 30 days to file a response. If they agree with the terms, this step goes quickly.
  4. Exchange financial disclosures. Both spouses share details of their income, assets and debts. Organized records make this step much faster.
  5. Reach agreement. Property, support and parenting arrangements are settled and written up, often as a marital settlement agreement drafting service would prepare.
  6. Submit the judgment. The court reviews your paperwork. If everything is complete and correct, a judge signs it, and once the waiting period has passed, you are officially divorced.

What makes a divorce faster or slower?

Several factors have an outsized effect on timing:

  • Cooperation. When both spouses agree often finishes close to the six-month minimum, though court backlogs vary by county.
  • Complexity. Businesses, real estate and retirement accounts take longer to value and divide.
  • Children. Custody and support arrangements need careful, detailed agreements.
  • Mistakes. Missing signatures, wrong forms or incomplete disclosures can send paperwork back for correction.
  • Disputes. Contested cases can take a year or more, sometimes much longer.

Ways to avoid unnecessary delays

  • Gather financial documents before you file
  • Agree on the big issues early, using divorce mediation services if you get stuck
  • Double-check every form before submitting it
  • Respond promptly to requests from the court or your spouse

Waiting does not mean standing still

The six months are not wasted time. Use them to separate finances, update beneficiaries, plan housing and settle into your new routine. A well-prepared case also reaches the finish line without last-minute surprises, which is can keep both the budget and the stress under control.
